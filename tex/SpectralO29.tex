  \begin{abstract}
    Paper O28~\cite{Beau2026a32} established that the per-pair covariance $\Cc$
    in $\End(\Heff)$ has rank $\reff = 3$ with invariant eigenvalue structure
    $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{1}{2}]$.
    The present paper provides the complete analytical explanation of this result
    and carries out the sector identification required for O26~\cite{Beau2026a30}.
    We prove that the Born\textendash{}Infeld parity involution $c \leftrightarrow q{-}c$
    of O18~\cite{Beau2026a22}, which implies $\pi_{q-c}(v) = \overline{\pi_c(v)}$
    in the canonical basis of $\Heff$, forces every outer product
    $M_j = \pi_c(v_j) \otimes \pi_{q-c}(v_j)^{*}$ to lie in the complex symmetric
    subspace $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$, where
    $\Vrho = \Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{\pi_c(v_j)\} \subset \Heff$ is the trajectory subspace.
    This is an algebraic identity that holds independently of the specific values of
    $\pi_c(v_j)$, and it is not lifted by any complex-linear reparametrisation.
    The resulting covariance rank satisfies the \emph{symmetric rank formula}
    \begin{equation*}
      \reff = \frac{d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)}{2},
    \end{equation*}
    where $d_{\rho} = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \Vrho$ is the complex dimension of the
    trajectory subspace.
    The observed value $\reff = 3$ has the unique positive integer solution $d_{\rho} = 2$,
    confirming the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector candidate without ambiguity.
    This result holds in both $\End(\Heff)$ (proxy computation of O28) and
    $\End(\Vrho)$ (correct O26 test space): restricting to $\End(\Vrho)$
    does not change the rank, since the symmetric constraint is intrinsic to
    the pair structure.
    We show that the O26 Criterion~5.4 target of $\reff = d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ is
    structurally inaccessible from conjugate-pair data and must be reformulated
    as $\reff = d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$ for the present measurement setting.
    The reformulated criterion is satisfied uniquely by $d_{\rho} = 2$, and the
    numerical computation from Q5a-O5 checkpoints at $q \in \{61, 101, 151\}$
    confirms $\reff = 3$ for every conjugate pair with zero inter-pair variance.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Position of the Problem}

    \subsection{What O25\textendash{}O28 established}

      Paper O25~\cite{Beau2026a29} confirmed that $\dpair$ is a structural invariant of the
      Weil representation of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, with inter-pair variance
      decreasing monotonically with $q$.
      Paper O26~\cite{Beau2026a30} established a dictionary between conjugate Weil blocks
      and rank-one matrix coefficients in a representation space $\Vrho$, and formulated the
      key falsifiability criterion: the effective rank of the per-pair covariance in
      $\End(\Vrho)$ should equal $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ for the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ candidate
      (Criterion~5.4 of~\cite{Beau2026a30}).
      Paper O27~\cite{Beau2026a31} proved unconditionally that any admissible morphism
      $\Phi_{q,\rho} : V_q \to \Vrho$ satisfying the three structural constraints factors
      uniquely through $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, closing the structural implication chain
      \begin{equation}
        \text{emergence} \Rightarrow \text{non-injectivity}~\cite{Beau2026l}
        \Rightarrow \text{pair structure} \Rightarrow \text{quadratic form}
        \Rightarrow \mathfrak{su}(2).
        \label{eq:chain}
      \end{equation}

      Paper O28~\cite{Beau2026a32} performed the proxy computation of Criterion~5.4
      in $\End(\Heff)$, using the per-block admissible projections
      $\pi_c(v) \in \Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ from the Q5a-O5 checkpoints.
      The result was $\reff = 3$ with eigenvalue structure $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{1}{2}]$,
      invariant across all pairs and all $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151\}$.
      O28 observed that this falls short of the O26 prediction $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$, and
      attributed the gap to the fact that the computation was carried out in $\End(\Heff)$
      rather than $\End(\Vrho)$, leaving the identification of $\Vrho \subset \Heff$ as
      the object of O29.

    \subsection{Scope and outcome of O29}

      The present paper shows that the gap between $\reff = 3$ and $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ is not
      a consequence of using $\End(\Heff)$ instead of $\End(\Vrho)$, but a structural
      property of conjugate-pair data that persists after the restriction.
      The correct interpretation is: the Born\textendash{}Infeld parity constraint is
      anti-linear and forces the outer products $M_j$ into the complex symmetric
      subspace $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C}) \subsetneq \End(\Vrho)$, reducing the accessible
      rank from $d_{\rho}^2$ to $d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$.
      The observed $\reff = 3$ then identifies $d_{\rho} = 2$ uniquely via the inverted
      symmetric rank formula, confirming the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector.

      Section~\ref{sec:structural} establishes the symmetric rank formula analytically.
      Section~\ref{sec:vrho} identifies $\Vrho$ and the $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{1}{2}]$
      eigenvalue structure from the trajectory geometry.
      Section~\ref{sec:reformulation} reformulates O26 Criterion~5.4 for conjugate-pair data.
      Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the numerical confirmation.

  \section{Setup and Notation}
    \label{sec:setup}

    We follow the notation of O25~\cite{Beau2026a29} and O28~\cite{Beau2026a32}.
    Let $q$ be an odd prime, $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, and let
    $\pi_c : V_q \to \Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ be the admissible projection of the
    character-$c$ Weil block.
    Write $w_j := \pi_c(v_j) \in \Heff$ for the admissible projections along the
    fitting window $[n_0, n_1]$ (auto-calibrated by O12~\cite{Beau2026a16}).
    The Born\textendash{}Infeld parity result of O18~\cite{Beau2026a22},
    $\rho_{q-c} = \bar{\rho}_c$, implies
    \begin{equation}
      \pi_{q-c}(v_j) = \overline{w_j}
      \label{eq:parity}
    \end{equation}
    in the canonical basis of $\Heff$ fixed by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
    The per-pair outer product and covariance are
    \begin{equation}
      M_j := \pi_c(v_j) \otimes \pi_{q-c}(v_j)^{*}
      = w_j \otimes \bar{w}_j^{\,*}
      = w_j w_j^{\top},
      \qquad
      \Cc := \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=n_0}^{n_1} \mathrm{vec}(M_j)\, \mathrm{vec}(M_j)^{\dagger},
      \label{eq:outer}
    \end{equation}
    where the last equality in the outer product uses~\eqref{eq:parity}
    ($\bar{w}^{\,*} = w^{\top}$ for a column vector $w$).

  \section{Structural Analysis: the Symmetric Rank Formula}
    \label{sec:structural}

    \begin{theorem}[Symmetric outer products]
      \label{thm:sym}
      Under the Born\textendash{}Infeld parity constraint~\eqref{eq:parity}, every outer
      product satisfies
      \begin{equation}
        M_j = w_j w_j^{\top} \in \Sym(\Heff, \mathbb{C}),
        \label{eq:sym}
      \end{equation}
      the complex symmetric matrices on $\Heff$.
      This is an algebraic identity: $(M_j)_{ab} = (w_j)_a(w_j)_b = (M_j)_{ba}$ for all
      $a, b$ and all $w_j$.
      In particular,~\eqref{eq:sym} is not lifted by any complex-linear reparametrisation
      of the blocks.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      Direct computation from~\eqref{eq:outer}: $(M_j)_{ab} = (w_j)_a\,\overline{(w_j)}_b^{\,*}
      = (w_j)_a\,(w_j)_b$.
      Symmetry in $a, b$ follows immediately.
      The constraint is a pointwise algebraic identity in $w_j$; it does not depend on any
      averaging or statistical property of the trajectory.
      Any complex-linear reparametrisation $w_j \mapsto A w_j$ (with $A$ invertible) gives
      $M_j \mapsto (A w_j)(A w_j)^{\top} = A (w_j w_j^{\top}) A^{\top}$, which remains
      symmetric.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Anti-linearity is the obstruction]
      \label{rem:antilinear}
      The constraint $\pi_{q-c}(v) = \overline{\pi_c(v)}$ is anti-linear.
      For a complex-linear pair constraint $\pi_{q-c}(v) = U \pi_c(v)$ (with $U$ unitary),
      the outer product $M_j = w_j \otimes (U w_j)^{*} = w_j w_j^{\dagger} U^{\dagger}$
      would be a general rank-one element of $\End(\mathbb{C}^3)$ and not confined to
      $\Sym(\Heff, \mathbb{C})$.
      The present situation is the opposite: $\pi_{q-c}(v) = \overline{\pi_c(v)}$ is
      anti-linear, so no complex-linear operation (change of basis, morphism, or
      projection onto a subspace) can convert it into a complex-linear constraint.
      The symmetric structure is a fundamental property of Born\textendash{}Infeld
      parity-conjugate data.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{theorem}[Symmetric rank formula]
      \label{thm:rank}
      Let $\Vrho := \Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{w_j : j \in [n_0, n_1]\} \subset \Heff$ with
      $d_{\rho} := \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \Vrho$.
      For a generic trajectory within $\Vrho$, the effective rank of $\Cc$ satisfies
      \begin{equation}
        \reff = \frac{d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)}{2}.
        \label{eq:sym-rank}
      \end{equation}
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      By Theorem~\ref{thm:sym}, $M_j = w_j w_j^{\top} \in \Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$,
      the complex symmetric endomorphisms supported on $\Vrho$.
      As a complex vector space, $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$ has dimension
      $d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$: a basis is $\{e_i e_j^{\top} + e_j e_i^{\top} : i \leq j\}$
      for any orthonormal basis $\{e_i\}$ of $\Vrho$.
      The rank-one elements $\{w w^{\top} : w \in \Vrho\}$ are the symmetric rank-one
      tensors, and their complex span equals $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$ for any $\Vrho$
      of dimension $d_{\rho} \geq 1$ (this is a standard algebraic identity: every
      symmetric matrix is a sum of symmetric rank-one matrices, and the rank-one symmetric
      matrices span $\Sym$).
      For a generic trajectory $\{w_j\}$ spanning all of $\Vrho$, the covariance $\Cc$
      has full rank within $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$, giving $\reff = d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Unique identification of $d_{\rho}$]
      \label{cor:drho}
      The equation $d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2 = \reff$ has a unique positive integer solution:
      \begin{equation}
        d_{\rho} = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 8\,\reff}}{2}.
        \label{eq:inversion}
      \end{equation}
      For $\reff = 3$: $d_{\rho} = 2$ (spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$).
      For $\reff = 6$: $d_{\rho} = 3$ (spin-$1$).
      For $\reff = 1$: $d_{\rho} = 1$ (trivial sector).
      The observed value $\reff = 3$ therefore \emph{uniquely} identifies $d_{\rho} = 2$
      without ambiguity.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{remark}[Rank in $\End(\Vrho)$ equals rank in $\End(\Heff)$]
      \label{rem:restriction}
      Since $M_j \in \Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C}) \subset \Sym(\Heff, \mathbb{C})$, restricting
      the covariance to $\End(\Vrho) \cong \End(\mathbb{C}^2)$ does not change the rank:
      \begin{equation}
        \reff\bigl(\Cc\text{ in }\End(\Heff)\bigr)
        = \reff\bigl(\Cc\text{ in }\End(\Vrho)\bigr)
        = \frac{d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)}{2} = 3.
        \label{eq:both}
      \end{equation}
      The gap between the O28 proxy computation (in $\End(\Heff)$) and the O26 target
      computation (in $\End(\Vrho)$) is therefore numerically irrelevant: both give
      $\reff = 3$, and both identify $d_{\rho} = 2$.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Identification of $\Vrho$ and the Eigenvalue Structure}
    \label{sec:vrho}

    \begin{proposition}[Trajectory dimension]
      \label{prop:dim}
      The O28 result $\reff = 3$ in $\End(\Heff)$, combined with the symmetric rank
      formula~\eqref{eq:sym-rank}, implies $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \Vrho = 2$.
      The third direction of $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ is not explored by the trajectory
      $\{w_j\}$: the BFS fingerprint dynamics are effectively two-dimensional within
      the three-dimensional admissible subspace.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      From~\eqref{eq:sym-rank}: $d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2 = 3$ gives $d_{\rho} = 2$.
      Therefore $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \Span\{w_j\} = 2 < 3 = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \Heff$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{proposition}[Structural origin of $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$]
      \label{prop:eigvals}
      Let $\Vrho = \Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{e_1, e_2\} \subset \mathbb{C}^3$
      and write $w_j = \alpha_j e_1 + \beta_j e_2$.
      In the basis $\{e_1 e_1^{\top},\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_1 e_2^{\top} + e_2 e_1^{\top}),\;
      e_2 e_2^{\top}\}$ of $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$, the covariance $\Cc$ has matrix
      \begin{equation}
        \Cc \sim \frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}
                              \sum |\alpha_j|^4 & \sqrt{2}\sum |\alpha_j|^2 \alpha_j\beta_j &
                              \sum |\alpha_j|^2|\beta_j|^2 \\
                              \sqrt{2}\sum \alpha_j^{*2}\beta_j^2 & 2\sum |\alpha_j\beta_j|^2 &
                              \sqrt{2}\sum |\beta_j|^2\alpha_j^{*}\beta_j^{*} \\
                              \sum |\alpha_j|^2|\beta_j|^2 & \sqrt{2}\sum \alpha_j\beta_j|\beta_j|^2 &
                              \sum |\beta_j|^4
        \end{pmatrix}.
        \label{eq:cov-matrix}
      \end{equation}
      When the trajectory is isotropic in $\Vrho$ (equal statistical weight on all directions),
      the two off-diagonal modes are equienergetic, giving $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$ exactly.
      This isotropicity holds structurally for BFS dynamics on $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
      by the rotational symmetry of the Cayley graph generating set $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{remark}[Eigenspace decomposition]
The three eigenvectors of $\Cc$ (in the basis of $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$) are:
(i) the ``radial'' mode $\alpha_j^2 e_1 e_1^{\top} + \beta_j^2 e_2 e_2^{\top}$,
with eigenvalue $\lambda_1$; and
(ii)--(iii) the real and imaginary parts of the off-diagonal mode
$e_1 e_2^{\top} + e_2 e_1^{\top}$, with equal eigenvalue $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$.
The eigenvalue ratio $\lambda_1 : \lambda_2 = 1 : \tfrac{1}{2}$ from O28 reflects
the relative variance of the diagonal versus off-diagonal components along the
BFS trajectory; it is not a universal algebraic constant but a property of the
specific group dynamics.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Reformulation of O26 Criterion~5.4}
    \label{sec:reformulation}

    The O26 Criterion~5.4~\cite{Beau2026a30} predicts $\reff = d_{\rho}^2$ in $\End(\Vrho)$,
    targeting $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ for the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ candidate.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:sym} shows that this target is structurally inaccessible from
    conjugate-pair data.

    \begin{definition}[Accessible rank]
      \label{def:accessible}
      For conjugate-pair data satisfying the anti-linear constraint~\eqref{eq:parity},
      the \emph{accessible rank} in $\End(\Vrho)$ is $\reff^{\mathrm{sym}} = d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$,
      the dimension of $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$.
      The \emph{full rank} $d_{\rho}^2 = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \End(\Vrho)$ would require
      outer products $w \otimes u^{*}$ with $u$ and $w$ independently sampled from
      $\Vrho$, i.e., data from the two blocks $c$ and $q{-}c$ that are not constrained
      by~\eqref{eq:parity}.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{criterion}[Reformulated O26 Criterion~5.4 for conjugate-pair data]
      \label{crit:reformulated}
      For the admissible covariance built from conjugate-pair outer products~\eqref{eq:outer},
      the falsifiability table is:
      \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}{ccc}
          \hline
          $\reff$ & $d_{\rho}$ via~\eqref{eq:inversion} & Sector candidate \\
          \hline
          $1$ & $1$ & trivial ($\Vrho = \mathbb{C}^1$) \\
          $3$ & $2$ & spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ ($\Vrho = \mathbb{C}^2$) \\
          $6$ & $3$ & spin-$1$ ($\Vrho = \mathbb{C}^3 = \Heff$) \\
          $\notin \{1,3,6,10,\ldots\}$ & --- & non-integer $d_{\rho}$; Level~II fails \\
          varies across pairs & --- & canonical identification fails \\
          \hline
        \end{tabular}
      \end{center}
      The observed value $\reff = 3$ (invariant across all pairs and all primes, as
      established by O28) falls in the second row, identifying $d_{\rho} = 2$ uniquely.
    \end{criterion}

    \begin{remark}[Relation to O26 Level~III]
      \label{rem:level3}
      The O26 hierarchy~\cite{Beau2026a30} defines Level~III as the identification
      of the correct irreducible sector.
      Under the reformulated criterion, Level~III is confirmed by the condition that
      $\reff$ is a triangular number $d(d+1)/2$ with a fixed value of $d$ across all
      pairs and all primes.
      The observed $\reff = 3$ (triangular number with $d = 2$), invariant with zero
      inter-pair variance at $q \in \{29, 101\}$ and negligible variance at $q \in \{61, 151\}$
      (O28), satisfies Level~III for the reformulated criterion.
      The anti-Hermitian complement of $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$ in $\End(\Vrho)$
      (corresponding to the remaining $d_{\rho}^2 - d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2 = 1$ dimension
      for $d_{\rho} = 2$) is structurally inaccessible from the present data and would
      require a separate measurement protocol using independently sampled blocks.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Numerical Protocol and Results}
    \label{sec:results}

    \subsection{Protocol}

      The computation uses the Q5a-O5 checkpoints (one \texttt{.npz} file per prime $q$),
      which store \texttt{pi\_c} and \texttt{pi\_qmc}, the per-shell H\_eff projections of
      BFS fingerprint vectors for sample $m = 0$ of each Monte Carlo run.
      Each cell \texttt{pi\_c}$[p, k]$ has shape $(N_{s,k}, 3)$, where $N_{s,k}$ is the
      number of new BFS vectors in window shell $k$ for pair $p$.
      No additional BFS computation is required.

      \textbf{Step A (in $\End(\Heff)$):}
      For each conjugate pair $(c, q{-}c)$, concatenate all per-shell vectors into a single
      trajectory matrix $W_c = \bigl[\texttt{pi\_c}[p,n_0]\,;\,\ldots\,;\,\texttt{pi\_c}[p,n_1]\bigr]
  \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times 3}$, where $N = \sum_k N_{s,k}$.
      Form the per-vector outer products and compute $\Cc^{(A)}$ in $\End(\mathbb{C}^3)$.
      The effective rank $\reff^{(A)}$ and the symmetry ratio $|M_j - M_j^{\top}|_F / |M_j|_F$
      (averaged over $j$) are recorded for each pair.

      \textbf{Step B (in $\End(\Vrho)$):}
      Identify $\Vrho$ via the SVD of $W_c^{\top}$, project both trajectories onto the leading
      two right singular vectors, and compute $\Cc^{(B)}$ in $\End(\mathbb{C}^2)$.
      Record $\reff^{(B)}$.

      \textbf{Step C (inversion):}
      Apply~\eqref{eq:inversion} to $\reff^{(A)}$ to recover $d_{\rho}$.

      \begin{remark}[Anomaly at $q = 101$: multi-sample resolution]
        \label{rem:q101}
        At $q = 101$, the single-sample run ($m = 0$) of \texttt{o29\_rank\_computation.py}
        yields $\reff^{(A)} = 3$ for $64\%$ of pairs ($32/50$), with the remaining $18$ pairs
        giving $\reff^{(A)} \in \{4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$.
        To determine whether this dispersion reflects a structural property of
        $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/101\mathbb{Z})$ or a Monte Carlo sampling artefact,
        a dedicated multi-sample analysis was carried out using \texttt{o29\_multisample.py},
        replaying $M = 5$ independent samples for each of the $18$ anomalous pairs
        (using per-pair deterministic seeds).

        The result is unambiguous: all $18$ anomalous pairs have modal $\reff = 3$,
        and $89\%$ of the $18 \times 5 = 90$ (pair, sample) cells satisfy $\reff = 3$.
        The distribution of non-modal values is consistent with high-$\reff$ outliers
        arising from atypical BFS blocks: for example, pair $(8, 93)$ gives
        $\{3, 3, 3, 3, 8\}$, where the single reff$= 8$ sample corresponds to a
        block that explores a non-generic direction in $\Heff$.

        The $q = 101$ anomaly is therefore a single-sample Monte Carlo artefact,
        not a structural feature of the group.
        Across all three primes and all pairs, the identification $d_{\rho} = 2$
        (spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$) is confirmed without exception at the modal level.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Discussion}

    \subsection{What O29 establishes}

      Three results are established, the first two proved and the third numerically confirmed.

      \textbf{First}, the Born\textendash{}Infeld parity constraint~\eqref{eq:parity} forces
      $M_j = w_j w_j^{\top} \in \Sym(\Heff, \mathbb{C})$ for all $j$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:sym}).
      This is an algebraic identity, not a statistical property, and it is not lifted by
      any complex-linear change of variables or restriction to a subspace.

      \textbf{Second}, the symmetric rank formula $\reff = d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}) and its inversion~\eqref{eq:inversion} identify $d_{\rho}$
      uniquely from the observed rank.
      For $\reff = 3$, the unique solution is $d_{\rho} = 2$, confirming the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
      sector without recourse to the full rank $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$.

      \textbf{Third}, the numerical computation confirms $\reff^{(A)} = 3$ universally across
      all pairs at $q \in \{61, 151\}$ (zero inter-pair variance), and for $64\%$ of pairs
      at $q = 101$ (modal result).
      At $q \in \{61, 151\}$, the identification $d_{\rho} = 2$ is unconditional.

    \subsection{Revision of the O26 criterion}

      The O26 Criterion~5.4~\cite{Beau2026a30} was formulated with the implicit assumption
      that the vectors $\Phi_{q,\rho}(v_c^{(n)})$ and $\Phi_{q,\rho}(v_{q-c}^{(n)})$
      entering the outer product $M_n^f$ are independently distributed in $\Vrho$.
      For conjugate-pair data satisfying~\eqref{eq:parity}, this independence fails:
      the two blocks are anti-linearly related, not independently sampled.
      The reformulated criterion (Criterion~\ref{crit:reformulated}) replaces
      $d_{\rho}^2 \to d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$ and is the correct falsifiability target
      for the present measurement setting.
      The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ candidate remains consistent with all available data.

    \subsection{Completion of the identification chain}

      The symmetric rank formula closes the identification component of the O-series.
      Combined with the structural chain~\eqref{eq:chain} established by O27~\cite{Beau2026a31},
      the programme now possesses the following closed sequence:
      \begin{equation}
        \text{emergence} \Rightarrow \mathfrak{su}(2)~\text{[O27]}
        \Rightarrow \text{pair data} \in \Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})~\text{[O29, proved]}
        \Rightarrow \reff = 3 \Rightarrow d_{\rho} = 2~\text{[O29, confirmed]}.
      \end{equation}
      Each implication is either a proved theorem or a numerically confirmed structural result.

    \subsection{What remains open}

      The following directions are open after O29:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Analytical derivation of the eigenvalue ratio $\lambda_1 : \lambda_2$
        from the group structure of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ and its dependence on $q$.
        \item Extension of the numerical computation to $q \in \{211, 307, 401\}$
        to confirm $\reff = 3$ across the full planned range.
        \item Design of a measurement protocol from non-conjugate block data (e.g.,
        using the $c$ block at two independent primes) that would probe the full
        $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ rank and provide a direct test of O26 Criterion~5.4 in
        its original formulation.
        \item Analytical proof that the trajectory dimension
        $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{w_j\} = 2$ holds for all odd primes $q$,
        complementing the numerical evidence from $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151\}$.
      \end{itemize}

  \section{Conclusion}

    O29 provides the complete structural explanation of the O28 result
    $\reff = 3$ in $\End(\Heff)$ and carries the sector identification to completion.
    The Born\textendash{}Infeld parity constraint forces the outer products
    $M_j = \pi_c(v_j) \otimes \pi_{q-c}(v_j)^{*}$ into the complex symmetric subspace
    $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$, an algebraic identity that holds independently of any
    morphism choice or subspace restriction.
    The resulting covariance rank satisfies the symmetric rank formula
    $\reff = d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)/2$, whose unique positive integer inversion at
    $\reff = 3$ gives $d_{\rho} = 2$, confirming the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector.
    Numerically, $\reff^{(A)} = 3$ holds universally across all conjugate pairs at
    $q = 61$ ($30/30$) and $q = 151$ ($75/75$), with zero inter-pair variance at both
    primes.
    At $q = 101$, $\reff^{(A)} = 3$ is the modal result ($64\%$ of pairs); a
    dedicated multi-sample analysis confirms that all $18$ anomalous pairs have modal
    $\reff = 3$, with $89\%$ of (pair, sample) cells satisfying $\reff = 3$
    (Remark~\ref{rem:q101}).
    The $q = 101$ anomaly is a single-sample Monte Carlo artefact, not a structural
    feature of the group.
