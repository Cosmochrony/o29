  \begin{abstract}
    Paper O28~\cite{Beau2026a32} established that the per-pair covariance $\Cc$ in $\End(\Heff)$ has rank $\reff = 3$
    with invariant eigenvalue structure $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{1}{2}]$.
    The present paper gives the structural explanation of this result.
    The value $\reff = 3$ is real and stable across primes; the conjugation identity
    $\rho_{q-c} = \bar{\rho}_c$ (an O17 theorem, recalled in O18 Theorem~3.1~\cite{Beau2026a22})
    acts anti-linearly, so the conjugate-pair outer products
    $M_j = \pi_c(v_j) \otimes \pi_{q-c}(v_j)^{*}$ are (up to a small, $q$-decreasing defect) complex symmetric; and the
    O26 target $\reff = d_\rho^2 = 4$ is structurally inaccessible from conjugate-pair data.
    A direct characterisation on the O25/Q5a checkpoints shows that the trajectory
    $\{w_j = \pi_c(v_j)\}$ spans \emph{all three} complex dimensions of $\Heff$ (singular ratios
    $\sigma_3/\sigma_1 \simeq 0.7\text{--}1.0$), and that $\reff = 3$ is a \emph{hard $6 \to 3$ collapse} of the
    Veronese (squaring) image: the symmetric squares $w_j \otimes w_j$ satisfy exactly three independent quadratic
    relations.
    The three constraint forms are traceless, and their commutators span exactly $\mathfrak{so}(3)$; the Schur
    commutant of the action on $\Heff$ is one-dimensional, so $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ is \emph{irreducible}.
    This identifies $\reff = 3$ as the invariant of the \emph{adjoint} action $\Heff \simeq \su(2)$ of O23/O27 ---
    the spin-$1$ (vector) representation.
    Since the adjoint is irreducible, $\Heff$ contains no two-dimensional invariant subspace: the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
    space $\Vrho$ is the \emph{Schur target} of the morphism $\bar\Phi : \su(2) \to \Vrho$ (O27), not a subspace of
    $\Heff$.
    Test~4 measures the adjoint carrier $\Heff \simeq \Sym^2(\Vrho)$, not $\Vrho$ directly: the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
    coordinate $\Vrho$ is recovered only through the Veronese square-root structure, not as the rank measured by
    $\reff$.
    Consequently Test~4 confirms the carrier (spin-$1$ on $\Heff$) and the inaccessibility of $d_\rho^2 = 4$, but does
    not by itself identify the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector; a genuine spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ test requires an independent
    observable on $\Vrho$ (the locking-broken protocol of Section~\ref{sec:results} reaches the full $d_\rho^2 = 4$ and
    motivates it).
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Position of the Problem}

    \subsection{What O25\textendash{}O28 established}

      Paper O25~\cite{Beau2026a29} confirmed that $\dpair$ is a structural invariant of the Weil representation of
      $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, with inter-pair variance decreasing monotonically with $q$.
      Paper O26~\cite{Beau2026a30} established a dictionary between conjugate Weil blocks and rank-one matrix
      coefficients in a representation space $\Vrho$, and formulated the key falsifiability criterion: the effective
      rank of the per-pair covariance in $\End(\Vrho)$ should equal $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ for the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
      candidate (Criterion~5.4 of~\cite{Beau2026a30}).
      Paper O27~\cite{Beau2026a31} proved unconditionally that any admissible morphism
      $\Phi_{q,\rho} : V_q \to \Vrho$ satisfying the three structural constraints factors uniquely through $\su(2)$,
      closing the structural implication chain
      \begin{equation}
        \text{emergence} \Rightarrow \text{non-injectivity}~\cite{Beau2026l}
        \Rightarrow \text{pair structure} \Rightarrow \text{quadratic form}
        \Rightarrow \su(2).
        \label{eq:chain}
      \end{equation}

      Paper O28~\cite{Beau2026a32} performed the proxy computation of Criterion~5.4 in $\End(\Heff)$, using the
      per-block admissible projections $\pi_c(v) \in \Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ from the Q5a-O5 checkpoints.
      The result was $\reff = 3$ with eigenvalue structure $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{1}{2}]$, invariant across all
      pairs and all $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151\}$.
      O28 observed that this falls short of the O26 prediction $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$, and left the interpretation of the
      value $3$ --- and of the relation between $\End(\Heff)$ and $\End(\Vrho)$ --- as the object of O29.

    \subsection{Scope and outcome of O29}
      \label{sec:scope}

      This paper explains the value $\reff = 3$ structurally and identifies what Test~4 measures.
      A direct characterisation on the checkpoints (Sections~\ref{sec:structural}\textendash{}\ref{sec:vrho}) shows
      that the trajectory $\{w_j\}$ spans all of $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$, and that the rank $3$ is a hard algebraic
      collapse of the Veronese squaring image whose constraint forms span $\mathfrak{so}(3)$.
      Combined with O23/O27, this identifies $\reff = 3$ as the invariant of the irreducible \emph{adjoint} action
      $\Heff \simeq \su(2)$ --- the spin-$1$ vector representation.
      The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ space $\Vrho$ is the Schur target of $\bar\Phi : \su(2) \to \Vrho$ and is not a subspace
      of $\Heff$; Test~4, which only sees data in $\Heff$, cannot identify it.

      The analysis rests on three facts: (i) $\reff = 3$ is real and stable; (ii) the Born\textendash{}Infeld parity
      is anti-linear and forces the conjugate-pair outer products into the symmetric subspace, up to a small,
      $q$-decreasing defect (Theorem~\ref{thm:sym}); (iii) the target $d_\rho^2 = 4$ is inaccessible from
      conjugate-pair data (Section~\ref{sec:reformulation}).

      Section~\ref{sec:structural} states the symmetric-square structure and the role of the trajectory dimension.
      Section~\ref{sec:vrho} characterises the variety carrying the $6 \to 3$ collapse and proves irreducibility.
      Section~\ref{sec:reformulation} reformulates O26 Criterion~5.4 as a carrier test.
      Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the numerical evidence.

  \section{Setup and Notation}
    \label{sec:setup}

    We follow the notation of O25~\cite{Beau2026a29} and O28~\cite{Beau2026a32}.
    Let $q$ be an odd prime, $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, and let $\pi_c : V_q \to \Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$
    be the admissible projection of the character-$c$ Weil block.
    Write $w_j := \pi_c(v_j) \in \Heff$ for the admissible projections along the fitting window $[n_0, n_1]$
    (auto-calibrated by O12~\cite{Beau2026a16}).
    The conjugation identity $\rho_{q-c} = \bar{\rho}_c$ (an O17 theorem, recalled in O18
    Theorem~3.1~\cite{Beau2026a22}) implies
    \begin{equation}
      \pi_{q-c}(v_j) = \overline{w_j}
      \label{eq:parity}
    \end{equation}
    in the canonical basis of $\Heff$ fixed by the supplied carrier of O23~\cite{Beau2026a27},
    up to a small phase defect quantified in
    Section~\ref{sec:results}.
    The per-pair outer product and covariance are
    \begin{equation}
      M_j := \pi_c(v_j) \otimes \pi_{q-c}(v_j)^{*}
      = w_j \otimes \bar{w}_j^{\,*}
      = w_j w_j^{\top},
      \qquad
      \Cc := \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=n_0}^{n_1} \mathrm{vec}(M_j)\, \mathrm{vec}(M_j)^{\dagger},
      \label{eq:outer}
    \end{equation}
    where the last equality in the outer product uses~\eqref{eq:parity} ($\bar{w}^{\,*} = w^{\top}$ for a column
    vector $w$).

  \section{Structural Analysis: Symmetric Squares and the Hypothesis that Fails}
    \label{sec:structural}

    \begin{theorem}[Symmetric outer products]
      \label{thm:sym}
      Under the Born\textendash{}Infeld parity constraint~\eqref{eq:parity}, every outer product satisfies
      \begin{equation}
        M_j = w_j w_j^{\top} \in \Sym(\Heff, \mathbb{C}),
        \label{eq:sym}
      \end{equation}
      the complex symmetric matrices on $\Heff$.
      This is an algebraic identity: $(M_j)_{ab} = (w_j)_a(w_j)_b = (M_j)_{ba}$ for all $a, b$ and all $w_j$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      Direct computation from~\eqref{eq:outer}: using the column-vector convention $\bar{w}^{\,*} = w^{\top}$,
      $(M_j)_{ab} = (w_j)_a\,(w_j)_b$, symmetric in $a, b$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Anti-linearity is the obstruction to $d_\rho^2$]
      \label{rem:antilinear}
      The constraint $\pi_{q-c}(v) = \overline{\pi_c(v)}$ is anti-linear.
      For a complex-linear pair constraint $\pi_{q-c}(v) = U \pi_c(v)$ (with $U$ unitary), the outer product
      $M_j = w_j \otimes (U w_j)^{*} = w_j w_j^{\dagger} U^{\dagger}$ would be a general rank-one element of
      $\End(\mathbb{C}^3)$.
      The present situation is the opposite: $\pi_{q-c}(v) = \overline{\pi_c(v)}$ is anti-linear, so no complex-linear
      operation converts it into a complex-linear constraint.
      This is the reason the original O26 target $d_\rho^2$ is unreachable from conjugate-pair data; see
      Section~\ref{sec:reformulation} and the locking-broken control of Section~\ref{sec:results}.
      Empirically the parity holds up to a small, $q$-decreasing defect (Section~\ref{sec:results}), so~\eqref{eq:sym}
      holds approximately rather than exactly.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{theorem}[Symmetric span of a trajectory]
      \label{thm:rank}
      Let $\Vrho := \Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{w_j\} \subset \Heff$ with $d_{\rho} := \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Vrho$.
      By Theorem~\ref{thm:sym} the symmetric squares $\{w_j w_j^{\top}\}$ lie in $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$, of
      dimension
      \begin{equation}
        \frac{d_{\rho}(d_{\rho}+1)}{2}.
        \label{eq:sym-rank}
      \end{equation}
      The effective rank $\reff$ therefore depends on the trajectory dimension $d_\rho$ and on the geometry of the
      squares within $\Sym(\Vrho, \mathbb{C})$.
      Both are read directly from the data in Section~\ref{sec:vrho}: $d_\rho = 3$
      ($\sigma_3/\sigma_1 \simeq 0.7\text{--}1.0$), and the squares occupy a three-dimensional,
      $\mathfrak{so}(3)$-structured subspace of the six-dimensional $\Sym(\Heff, \mathbb{C})$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{corollary}[The carrier is fixed by the $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ structure]
      \label{cor:drho}
      A bare value $\reff = 3$ does not by itself fix a representation dimension.
      The arithmetic map
      \begin{equation}
        d = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 8\,\reff}}{2}
        \label{eq:inversion}
      \end{equation}
      presumes that the squares fill $\Sym(\mathbb{C}^d)$ for a trajectory confined to $d$ dimensions, which is not
      the case here ($d_\rho = 3$, Proposition~\ref{prop:dim}).
      The carrier is fixed directly by the $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ structure of the constraint forms and the irreducibility
      of the action (Proposition~\ref{prop:so3}, Corollary~\ref{cor:adjoint}): $\reff = 3$ is the
      $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\su(2) = 3$ occurrence that O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} isolates among the distinct meanings of the
      number $3$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{remark}[Rank in $\End(\Vrho)$ versus $\End(\Heff)$]
      \label{rem:restriction}
      Projecting the trajectory onto the leading two singular directions of $\Heff$ and forming the covariance in
      $\End(\mathbb{C}^2)$ gives $\reff = 2$ (eigenvalue spectrum $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : 0 : 0]$) for every pair at
      $q \in \{29, 61\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
      The carrier rank $3$ is intrinsic to the full $\End(\Heff)$ and reflects the three-dimensional carrier; it is
      not reproduced in a two-dimensional subspace.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The Carrier: the Adjoint Spin-$1$ Representation on $\Heff$}
    \label{sec:vrho}

    \begin{proposition}[Trajectory dimension]
      \label{prop:dim}
      The trajectory $\{w_j\}$ spans all three complex dimensions of $\Heff$.
      On the checkpoints, the singular values of the trajectory matrix $W_c = [w_{n_0}, \ldots, w_{n_1}]$ satisfy
      $\sigma_2/\sigma_1, \sigma_3/\sigma_1 \in [0.7, 1.0]$, with $\rank_{10^{-2}} W_c = 3$ for every pair at
      $q \in \{29, 61\}$.
      Hence $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Span\{w_j\} = 3 = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Heff$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{remark}[Truncation caveat]
      \label{rem:truncation}
      The vectors $w_j = \pi_c(v_j)$ are the three-coordinate projections produced with
      $\mathrm{HEFF\_DIM} = 3$: $\pi_c$ is a truncation, and the rank of the ambient
      pre-saturation pipeline cannot be measured from these coordinates
      (Q5a-O2, Proposition~4.1).
      Proposition~\ref{prop:dim} is therefore a statement internal to the truncated space
      $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$: it shows that the trajectory fills the truncation, not that the
      ambient admissible dimension equals~$3$.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{theorem}[Hard $6 \to 3$ Veronese collapse]
      \label{thm:veronese}
      The symmetric squares $\{w_j \otimes w_j\}$ span a subspace of $\Sym(\Heff, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^6$ of
      dimension exactly $3$: the monomial matrix
      $\Phi$, with rows $\bigl(w_0^2, w_1^2, w_2^2, w_0 w_1, w_0 w_2, w_1 w_2\bigr)$, has singular spectrum
      $[1 : {\sim}0.9 : {\sim}0.9 : 0 : 0 : 0]$ with three machine-zero values.
      Equivalently the $w_j$ lie on the intersection of exactly three conics: there are three independent complex
      symmetric forms $B_1, B_2, B_3$ with $w_j^{\top} B_k w_j = 0$ for all $j$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}[Numerical proof]
      Computed on the pooled Q5a-O5 checkpoints (script \texttt{variety\_summary.py}), the three trailing singular
      values of $\Phi$ are at machine precision for $q \in \{29, 61\}$; the null space has dimension $6 - 3 = 3$.
      The identity form (the trace direction $w^{\top}w$) lies in the row space, not the null space (residual
      $5 \times 10^{-16}$), so $w^{\top}w$ is a coordinate of the rank-$3$ image, not a constraint.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{proposition}[The constraint forms span $\mathfrak{so}(3)$; the action is irreducible]
      \label{prop:so3}
      The three constraint forms $B_k$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:veronese} are traceless and rank-$2$ (eigenvalues
      $\{\mu, -\mu, 0\}$).
      Their commutators $L_k := [B_i, B_j]$ are exactly antisymmetric (residual $\sim 10^{-17}$) with eigenvalues
      $\{0, +i\mu', -i\mu'\}$, and $\Span\{L_1, L_2, L_3\} = \mathfrak{so}(3)$ exactly (fraction in
      $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ equal to $1.000$, rank $3$).
      The Schur commutant of $\{B_k\}$ (equivalently $\{L_k\}$) on $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ is one-dimensional
      (scalars only) at $q \in \{29, 61\}$.
      Hence the action is irreducible: $\Heff$ has no nontrivial invariant subspace, in particular no invariant
      two-dimensional subspace.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}[Numerical proof]
      Direct computation (script \texttt{variety\_summary.py}): traces of the $B_k$ vanish; the commutators are
      antisymmetric to machine precision and span $\mathfrak{so}(3)$; the linear system $[M, L_k] = 0$ for
      $k = 1, 2, 3$ has a one-dimensional solution space (the scalars) at both primes.
      Irreducibility over $\mathbb{C}$ is equivalent to a scalar commutant by Schur's lemma.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[$\Heff \simeq \su(2)$ is the spin-$1$ carrier]
      \label{cor:adjoint}
      By O23/O27~\cite{Beau2026a27,Beau2026a31} (conditionally on the supplied spinor carrier
      of O23), the admissible automorphism group of $\Heff$ is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
      acting on $\Heff \simeq \su(2)$ by the adjoint action; the adjoint is the three-dimensional irreducible (spin-$1$,
      vector) representation.
      Proposition~\ref{prop:so3} realises this action directly from the data: the $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ generators emerge
      as the commutators of the Veronese constraint forms, and the action is irreducible.
      Therefore $\reff = 3$ is the invariant of the spin-$1$ carrier $\Heff$, structural label: \emph{structural}
      (proved at the level of the data via Theorem~\ref{thm:veronese}\textendash{}Proposition~\ref{prop:so3}, and
      consistent with the categorical statement of O27, itself conditional on the supplied
      carrier of O23).
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proposition}[Structural origin of $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$]
      \label{prop:eigvals}
      The two-fold degeneracy $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$ of the $[1 : \tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{1}{2}]$ spectrum is the axial
      ($\mathrm{U}(1) \subset \mathrm{SO}(3)$) degeneracy of a spin-$1$ carrier with a preferred direction.
      The linear Hermitian covariance $\langle w\, w^{\dagger} \rangle$ has spectrum $[1 : 0.6 : 0.6]$ at $q = 29$ and
      $[1 : 0.8 : 0.8]$ at $q = 61$, with $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$ and a trend towards isotropy $[1:1:1]$ as $q$ grows.
      The eigenvalue ratio is not a universal algebraic constant but a property of the group dynamics on
      $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    \end{proposition}

  \section{Reformulation of O26 Criterion 5.4 as a Carrier Test}
    \label{sec:reformulation}

    The O26 Criterion~5.4~\cite{Beau2026a30} predicts $\reff = d_{\rho}^2$ in $\End(\Vrho)$, targeting
    $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ for the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ candidate.
    Two independent facts reshape this criterion.

    \begin{definition}[Accessible rank]
      \label{def:accessible}
      For conjugate-pair data satisfying the anti-linear constraint~\eqref{eq:parity}, the outer products are symmetric
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:sym}); the antisymmetric part of $\End(\Vrho)$ is never sampled.
      The \emph{full rank} $d_{\rho}^2$ would require outer products $w \otimes u^{*}$ with $u$ and $w$ independently
      sampled, i.e.\ data from the two blocks $c$ and $q{-}c$ that are not constrained by~\eqref{eq:parity}.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{criterion}[Reformulated O26 Criterion~5.4: carrier test]
      \label{crit:reformulated}
      Test~4, built from conjugate-pair outer products~\eqref{eq:outer}, measures a property of the \emph{carrier}
      $\Heff$, not of the target $\Vrho$.
      The operative readings are:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item $\reff = 3$ in $\End(\Heff)$, invariant across pairs and primes, with the trajectory spanning all of
        $\Heff$ and the constraint forms spanning $\mathfrak{so}(3)$: the carrier is the irreducible adjoint
        (spin-$1$) representation $\Heff \simeq \su(2)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:adjoint}).
        \item The original target $d_{\rho}^2 = 4$ is structurally inaccessible from conjugate-pair data
        (Definition~\ref{def:accessible}); it is recovered only by breaking the per-vector conjugate locking
        (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
      \end{itemize}
      The identification of the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ \emph{target} $\Vrho$ is not decided by Test~4: $\Vrho$ is the
      Schur target of $\bar\Phi : \su(2) \to \Vrho$ (O27), not a subspace of $\Heff$.
    \end{criterion}

    \begin{remark}[Relation to O26 Level~III]
      \label{rem:level3}
      The O26 hierarchy~\cite{Beau2026a30} defines Level~III as the identification of the correct irreducible sector.
      Test~4 confirms an irreducible carrier --- the spin-$1$ adjoint on $\Heff$ --- with $\reff = 3$ invariant
      (zero inter-pair variance at $q \in \{29, 61\}$, cf.\ O28).
      It does not confirm the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ \emph{target}; the choice $d_\rho = 2$ rests on the minimality
      argument of O26, not on $\reff$, and a direct test of $\Vrho$ requires the independent observable of
      Definition~\ref{def:accessible}.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Numerical Protocol and Results}
    \label{sec:results}

    \subsection{Protocol}

      All computations use the Q5a-O5 checkpoints (one \texttt{.npz} file per prime $q$), which store \texttt{pi\_c}
      and \texttt{pi\_qmc}, the per-shell $\Heff$ projections of BFS fingerprint vectors for sample $m = 0$.
      No additional BFS computation is required.
      Three scripts implement the analysis: \texttt{o29\_rank\_computation.py} (the $\reff$ measurement reproducing
      O28), \texttt{o29\_derisk\_diagnostic.py} (full spectra in $\End(\Heff)$ and $\End(\Vrho)$, parity defect, and
      the locking-broken control), and \texttt{variety\_summary.py} (the Veronese collapse, the constraint forms, and
      the Schur commutant).

      \textbf{Step A ($\End(\Heff)$).}
      For each conjugate pair, concatenate the per-shell vectors into $W_c \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times 3}$, form the
      symmetric outer products, and compute $\reff$ and the eigenvalue spectrum of $\Cc$ in $\End(\mathbb{C}^3)$.

      \textbf{Step B ($\End(\Vrho)$).}
      Project onto the leading two singular directions of $W_c$ and recompute $\reff$ in $\End(\mathbb{C}^2)$.

      \textbf{Step C (carrier).}
      Compute the Veronese monomial spectrum, the three constraint forms $B_k$, their commutators, and the Schur
      commutant, establishing irreducibility (Section~\ref{sec:vrho}).

      \textbf{Step D (control).}
      Recompute $\reff$ in $\End(\Vrho)$ after a random permutation of the $q{-}c$ index, breaking the per-vector
      conjugate locking while preserving the marginal distribution.

      \paragraph{Carrier structure (Step C).}
      The Veronese monomial spectrum is $[1 : {\sim}0.9 : {\sim}0.9 : 0 : 0 : 0]$ (three machine zeros) at

      covariance $\langle w\, w^{\dagger}\rangle$ has the axial spectrum $[1 : 0.6 : 0.6] \to [1 : 0.8 : 0.8]$
      ($q = 29 \to 61$).
      Figure~\ref{fig:variety} summarises the collapse and the $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ structure.

      \paragraph{Locking-broken control (Step D).}
      In $\End(\Vrho)$, the real conjugate data give $\reff = 2$ and the exact-parity surrogate
      $M_j = w_j \otimes w_j$ also gives $\reff = 2$, while breaking the per-vector locking by permuting the $q{-}c$
      index restores $\reff = 4 = d_\rho^2$ for every pair at $q \in \{29, 61\}$.
      This is the operational proof that $d_\rho^2 = 4$ is inaccessible from conjugate-pair data
      (Definition~\ref{def:accessible}), and it isolates an independent observable that does reach the full rank ---
      the natural starting point for a genuine future test of the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ target $\Vrho$.

  \section{Discussion}

    \subsection{What O29 establishes}

      Three results stand, with epistemic labels.

      \textbf{First (structural, proved on the data).}
      $\reff = 3$ is a hard $6 \to 3$ collapse of the Veronese squaring image of the $\Heff$ trajectory
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:veronese}); the three constraint forms span $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ and the action on
      $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ is irreducible (Proposition~\ref{prop:so3}).
      Combined with the proved categorical statement of O27, this identifies $\reff = 3$ as the invariant of the
      adjoint (spin-$1$) carrier $\Heff \simeq \su(2)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:adjoint}).

      \textbf{Second (proved).}
      The Born\textendash{}Infeld parity is anti-linear (Theorem~\ref{thm:sym}, Remark~\ref{rem:antilinear}); the
      target $d_\rho^2 = 4$ is inaccessible from conjugate-pair data, and is restored by the locking-broken control
      (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

      \textbf{Third (conditional).}
      The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ \emph{target} $\Vrho$ is the Schur target of $\bar\Phi : \su(2) \to \Vrho$ (O27) and is
      not a subspace of $\Heff$; Test~4 does not identify it.
      Its selection rests on the O26 minimality argument, pending an independent observable.

      \paragraph{Corrected status of Test~4.}
      Test~4 does not identify the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ carrier directly.
      It identifies the adjoint $\Heff \simeq \su(2)$ carrier, through a hard $6 \to 3$ collapse of the Veronese
      squaring image.
      The observed value $\reff = 3$ is therefore the spin-$1$/adjoint invariant already selected by the effective
      $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ automorphism structure, not the symmetric-rank inversion $3 = d_\rho(d_\rho+1)/2$ for
      $d_\rho = 2$.
      The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector remains the Schur target $V_\rho$, and requires an independent observable.

    \subsection{Revision of the O26 criterion}

      The O26 Criterion~5.4~\cite{Beau2026a30} was formulated with the implicit assumption that
      $\Phi_{q,\rho}(v_c^{(n)})$ and $\Phi_{q,\rho}(v_{q-c}^{(n)})$ are independently distributed in $\Vrho$.
      For conjugate-pair data this independence fails: the blocks are anti-linearly related.
      Criterion~\ref{crit:reformulated} replaces the target-space prediction by a carrier test on $\Heff$, and records
      that the original $d_\rho^2 = 4$ is reachable only with independently sampled blocks (Step~D).

    \subsection{Position in the identification chain}

      Relative to the chain~\eqref{eq:chain}, O29 makes precise where the data live:
      \begin{equation}
        \text{emergence} \Rightarrow \su(2)~\text{[O27]}
        \Rightarrow \Heff \simeq \su(2)~\text{(adjoint spin-$1$)}
        \Rightarrow \reff = 3.
      \end{equation}
      The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ target $\Vrho$ sits one arrow further, through $\bar\Phi : \su(2) \to \Vrho$, and is not
      reached by the conjugate-pair covariance.

    \subsection{What remains open}

      \begin{itemize}
        \item An analytic proof that the Veronese image collapses $6 \to 3$ for all odd primes $q$ (numerically
        verified at $q \in \{29, 61\}$), and that the constraint forms span $\mathfrak{so}(3)$.
        \item A canonical, independent observable on $\Vrho$ --- built from non-conjugate block data, as in the
        locking-broken control --- that would directly test the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ target and reach $d_\rho^2 = 4$.
        \item An analytic derivation of the axial ratio $\lambda_1 : \lambda_2$ and its dependence on $q$.
        \item Extension of the carrier diagnostics (Veronese collapse, $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ span, Schur commutant) to
        $q \in \{101, 151, 211, 307\}$.
      \end{itemize}

  \section{Conclusion}

    O29 explains the O28 result $\reff = 3$ in $\End(\Heff)$ and identifies what Test~4 measures.
    The value $3$ is real and stable, but it is the invariant of the irreducible \emph{adjoint} (spin-$1$)
    representation $\Heff \simeq \su(2)$: $\reff = 3$ arises from a hard $6 \to 3$ collapse of the Veronese squaring
    image of the trajectory, whose three constraint forms span $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ and whose action on
    $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ has a one-dimensional Schur commutant (irreducible).
    The trajectory spans all three dimensions of $\Heff$ and the rank in $\End(\Vrho)$ is $2$, so $\reff = 3$ is the
    carrier invariant of $\Heff$, not a symmetric rank $d_\rho(d_\rho+1)/2$ of a two-dimensional $\Vrho$.
    The Born\textendash{}Infeld parity is anti-linear, and the target $d_\rho^2 = 4$ is inaccessible from
    conjugate-pair data --- restored only by breaking the per-vector locking.
    The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector is therefore the Schur target $\Vrho$ of $\bar\Phi : \su(2) \to \Vrho$, not a
    subspace of $\Heff$, and its identification requires an independent observable, for which the locking-broken
    protocol provides the natural starting point.
    This keeps O29 consistent with O23's explicit warning against conflating the distinct occurrences of the number
    $3$~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
